Commercial Floor Cleaning and Strip and Wax in Troy, MI

What is commercial floor cleaning? It's cleaning and maintaining hard floors in businesses with machines built for big spaces and heavy traffic, instead of mops and buckets. Done right, it keeps floors safe to walk on, makes them last longer and keeps them looking new.

Most cleaning companies treat floors as an add-on to the nightly janitorial routine. At PeckPro, floors are the main job. In James's words, the machine work (auto scrubbers, burnishers, strip and wax) is "the work worth putting payroll behind." Floors are the first thing a customer sees when they walk in, and they're the hardest thing for a regular janitorial crew to get right with a mop.

  • Strip and wax for VCT and other waxed tile
  • Burnishing to bring back the shine between refinishes
  • Auto scrubbing for concrete and coated floors

Most of our floor work in Troy is done after close or on weekends. We work until 11 PM most nights, so a strip and wax can start after your last customer leaves and the floor has time to cure (fully harden) before the doors open again.

Which Floor Service Does Your Floor Need?

A dull floor doesn't always need the biggest job. The right service depends on what's actually wrong with it:

  • Burnishing when the floor still has good wax but looks dull. A high-speed machine polishes the wax back to a shine. It's quick, and it's the cheapest way to keep a floor looking new.
  • Strip and wax when the wax is yellow, scratched, patchy or worn through to the tile. We strip the old wax down to the tile and lay fresh coats.
  • Auto scrubbing for concrete, coated floors and the regular cleaning of big spaces, where the goal is clean and dry, not shiny.

Not sure which one you need? That's what the walk-through is for. We'll give you the honest answer, even when it's the cheaper one.

What we don't do: residential floors. We also don't take small offices, homes, restaurants or event venues.

What Happens During a Strip and Wax

A strip and wax is a series of steps, and skipping any of them is why some floors look great for a week and then fall apart. This is the order the work follows:

1

Clear and protect.

Movable items out; edges, walls and baseboards protected.

2

Strip.

Stripping solution and a floor machine break down the old wax all the way to the tile.

3

Rinse and dry.

The floor is cleaned of stripper residue and fully dried. New wax won't bond to a dirty or wet floor.

4

Wax.

Fresh coats laid in thin, even layers, each one dry before the next.

5

Cure.

The floor is left to fully harden before traffic.

6

Burnish (optional).

A high-speed polish for extra shine once the wax has cured.

When You Don't Need a Full Strip and Wax

A full strip and wax is the biggest floor job there is, and it isn't always the right one. You may not need it when:

  • The floor is dull but the wax is intact. A burnish brings it back for much less money and much less time.
  • Only the high-traffic lanes are worn. We can treat the worn areas, like entries and walkways, instead of the whole floor.
  • It's dirty, not damaged. A deep machine scrub may be all it needs to look right again.
  • You just had it done. A floor that was refinished recently usually needs regular burnishing, not another strip.

We'll tell you at the walk-through which one your floor needs.

What Drives the Cost of Commercial Floor Care

Floor work is priced by the floor, not by a flat rate, because the same square footage can take very different amounts of work. These move the number most:

  • Square footage. The biggest factor.
  • Floor type and condition. Heavy buildup or many old layers of wax take longer to strip.
  • Service. Burnishing costs much less than a full strip and wax.
  • Timing. After-hours and weekend work, and how much has to be moved first.

When you compare quotes, ask how many coats of wax are included, whether the floor is fully stripped to the tile or just "top scrubbed," and who to call if it doesn't come out right.
