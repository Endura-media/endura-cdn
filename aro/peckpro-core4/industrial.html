Manufacturing and Industrial Cleaning in Troy, MI

In a plant, a dirty floor is more than a looks problem. Dust, oil and debris on walkways and around equipment make floors slippery, and grime slowly covers the painted lines and safety markings people rely on to stay out of forklift paths. A mop pushes that grime around and leaves the floor wet, which is the last thing you want in a building with traffic moving through it.

PeckPro is built around floor machines. James put it plainly: auto scrubbers, burnishers and strip and wax are "the work worth putting payroll behind." On a plant floor, the auto scrubber does the heavy lifting: it puts down cleaning solution, scrubs the concrete or coating and vacuums the dirty water back up in the same pass, so the floor is clean and dry fast. Break rooms, locker areas, restrooms and elevators get regular cleaning so the people who work there have a clean place to take a break.

What Drives the Cost of Industrial Cleaning

Every plant is different, so we don't publish a price list. These are the things that move the number most:

Square footage and floor type. Sealed concrete, coated floors and tile each need different machines, pads and chemicals.

How often. Daily, weekly or monthly floor work. More often usually means less work each visit.

Access and timing. Work during shift changes, nights or weekends, and how much of the floor is clear when we arrive.

Condition today. Heavy oil or months of buildup takes more work the first time than it takes to maintain.

Questions worth asking any vendor you're comparing: Do you bring auto scrubbers, or mops? Who answers when there's a problem? Are you insured? How will you work around our shifts?
